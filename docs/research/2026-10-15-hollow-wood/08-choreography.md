# 08 — Choreography, rhythm, and optional sound

Research and independent challenge of `CONCEPTS.md`, 2026-10-15. Scope: the five provisional Dark Enchanted Forest / Spectral Creatures treatments. This is a timing treatment, not an animation review: no reference films were played, no animatic exists, and no claimed recognition time or finished-animation score has been measured.

**Recommendation:** retain the five different dramatic mechanisms, but stop treating 24 seconds as their common container. Trial cuts of **28 / 32 / 18 / 20 / 26 seconds** better expose their differences. These are authored starting hypotheses, not ideal durations established by research. The largest structural problems are the unearned wrong shadow in Procession, the competing actions during Crown's recognition, and resets that conceal a head but leave a changing torso visible.

## Five specific timing revisions

The times below describe attention and events, not five equally spaced phases. Fractions are trial edit points. Nothing should pulse at a shared six-second interval merely to make the set consistent.

### 1. Hollow Crown — trial 28 seconds; recognition, then contradiction

**0–3:** almost ordinary tree, broad fog behind it. **3–8:** one root takes weight and draws the shoulder into a recognizable configuration; let its shadow agree with this ordinary movement. **8–11:** reveal the three main antler forks and hollow chest. **11–13.3:** protect that readable stag pose; only settle weight and trailing fibers. **13.3:** the shadow begins its head turn. **14–16:** the head follows. **16–19:** hold its new attention toward the observer. **19–24:** a foreground bough sweeps across the head, shoulder, and root-leg in sequence as the anatomy folds back along corresponding landmarks. **24–28:** finish the fully covered reset and let the cover clear into the initial tree.

The key revision is a separate recognition hold before the shadow event. The proposed 0.7-second lead is deliberately testable, not a proven perceptual threshold. If observers see only poor synchronization, drop the discrepancy. If a covering bough cannot hide the whole changing anatomy, author the visible return as a coherent transformation; do not solve it with a general dissolve. Twenty-four seconds may ultimately suffice if the turn and cover read clearly; first remove idle fog time, not the recognition hold. The 28-second cut buys those comparisons without requiring a more elaborate creature action. [Timing](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/timing-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/), [anticipation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/anticipation-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).

### 2. Lantern Procession — trial 32 seconds; pattern, delay, absence

**0–4:** first lantern enters at the distant bend. **4–11:** two more travelers appear with unequal entry intervals, for example 2.1 and 3.4 seconds; trunks permit overlap without revealing three full figures together. **11–15:** the first traveler stops, then the second, then the third; their lanterns settle after their bodies. **15–18:** heads turn toward one clear empty patch, without another bright event elsewhere. **18–22:** carriers pass behind trunks. One lantern emerges alone. **22–26:** give the unsupported lantern a slow, readable continuation with empty space above it. **26–32:** it passes behind the near trunk while the next distant approach continues through the seam.

**Drop the wrong-way shadow.** The revision spends the saved attention on seeing that the light has no carrier. This is the clearest within-loop use of repetition: first teach two normal body/lantern relationships, then violate the third. Feet drive bodies; bodies drive lantern sway; sway settles after the halt. At 24 seconds, shorten the approach only if these comparisons survive. Do not rush the unsupported-lantern interval to preserve an arbitrary runtime. [Moving holds](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/why-all-animators-need-to-master-the-moving-hold/).

### 3. Murder of the Moon — trial 18 seconds; accumulation and collapse

**0–2:** one readable crow crosses the moon's edge. **2–6.5:** followers build one rising arc. **6.5–8:** the arc compresses across the moon. **8–10.5:** hold the impossible wing as a stable outer mass, allowing only restrained internal feather overlap and continued travel. **10.5–13:** its trailing edge breaks into leaves following the same tangents and momentum. **13–16:** canopy swallows the leaves; reveal the moon only as their coverage clears. **16–18:** restore the distant traffic behind that canopy while the opening crow approaches on its hidden path.

This event benefits from a shorter cut: the flock is a decisive collective gesture, and a prolonged preamble or leaf coda can turn it into ambience. Preserve the arc through both materials; do not change to a new vortex after the reveal. Repeated wingbeats belong inside the larger envelope, with varied phase and scale, not synchronized global flapping. If 18 seconds feels hurried, expand the gathered mass's readable hold before extending particle travel. [Timing](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/timing-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).

### 4. Mycelium Oracle — trial 20 seconds; arrival, scrutiny, discharge

**0–3:** one low signal approaches a junction. **3–6:** it takes three unequal branch routes in order; fibers respond after the signal reaches them. **6–8:** local bark tightens before the heavy lid opens. **8–11.5:** the eye is open and mostly still; the network dims behind it. **11.5–12.5:** one small tracking movement. **12.5–15:** let the new gaze register before closing. **15–17.5:** lid closes and stored tension releases outward. **17.5–20:** discharge into a lower knot; reset the hidden iris beneath the closed lid.

The revision gives the eye an uninterrupted first read, then a separate change of attention. Reset the iris while hidden, but leave any visible channels to complete a coherent discharge; do not reverse every growth motion. The three unequal signal arrivals form a phrase rather than identical flashing lanes. Twenty seconds suits this single encounter better than adding another four seconds of glow. [Anticipation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/anticipation-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/), [moving holds](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/why-all-animators-need-to-master-the-moving-hold/).

### 5. Forest Behind the Wall — trial 26 seconds; resisted force

**0–4:** roots grip boundary points and tense. **4–8:** one grip slips, exposing thickness and a narrow seam. **8–11:** a second, stronger pull opens depth with unequal edges. **11–15:** a partial shoulder crosses behind two internal trunks. **15–17:** hold the shoulder's near occlusion so its scale can register. **17–20:** the next step makes those trunks bow toward the opening; boundary roots recoil, then tighten. **20–23.5:** the attached edges close against that pressure. **23.5–26:** fully covered interior resets; the last exterior crease and its cast shadow settle into the initial panel.

The revision replaces a generic open-portal interval with resisted pulls and a visible response to approach. The exterior frame must keep acting while the creature remains partial. Do not add a second face reveal at closure. If this needs 30 seconds to communicate surface thickness, allow it; if attachment reads at 22, keep the shorter version. Duration follows the visible mechanics. [Anticipation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/anticipation-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).

## Silent-first delivery and optional sound

Current source verification matters: `src/model.cpp:36` creates audio muted; `src/main.cpp:154–155` provides **Mute audio** and toggles the selected source's mute state. The inspector reads that state at line 387. Thus the accurate statement is **video audio is muted by default and may be enabled manually**. This source inspection does not prove persisted audio settings, actual speaker playback, or separate-file synchronization; those were not tested here.

Every core cause has a visible carrier: Crown's weight transfer, Procession's body-light separation, the moon's actual covering mass, Oracle's arriving signal and tissue response, and Wall's gripping roots. Do not use a bell to explain an otherwise unmotivated collective turn or an offscreen footstep as the only evidence of the tall presence. The turn can remain ambiguous because bodies visibly adopt the same attention.

If sound is later commissioned, plan a sparse optional version while blocking: one timber strain before Crown's weight release; an irregular chain/footfall phrase that ceases while the orphan lantern continues; a broad wing rush thinning into dry leaves; a woody pressure sound that stops during Oracle's held gaze; and a tension scrape tied to Wall's roots. Leave gaps. These are original suggestions, not observed cues from the cited films. The picture must pass muted first; then audition whether the optional track changes perceived pacing or points attention away from the intended image. That distinction follows the delivery constraints while taking sound's storytelling role seriously. [Thom's original talk](https://www.philipbrophy.com/projects/cinesonic/randythom.html).

For one composite show, a single authored soundtrack is easier to reason about than five overlapping copies of the same ambience. Actual multi-surface audio behavior needs a separate playback check; do not claim it from this timing plan.

## Evidence to request from the first animatic

1. Show the five silent cuts separately, including entry midway through each loop. Record what a viewer identifies before explaining the intended creature. A single successful prompted viewing is insufficient evidence of an unprompted reveal.
2. Compare each proposal with a cut that borrows time from its approach and gives it to its recognition hold. Ask whether it clarifies the action or merely feels slow. Do not assume a longer hold is always better.
3. Observe at least three consecutive joins at normal playback. Check a visible silhouette landmark, its cast shadow, fog direction, brightness, and foreground cover across the seam. Matching first/last stills alone cannot establish continuous velocity or decode continuity.
4. For repetition, distinguish a within-scene pattern from a file repeating: the travelers' behavior must teach the absence; every loop need not introduce a new surprise. Check whether anticipation becomes tedious on repeated viewing without speeding up the key readable event.
5. Obtain the separate intended-size projector judgment. Thumbnail, lifted-black, and desktop playback checks can reject weak staging; they cannot establish wall readability.

No finished treatment passes motion, seam, projector, or audio acceptance on the strength of prose. Crown remains the strongest protagonist-led proof; Oracle is the cleanest short comparison; Procession currently needs the most editorial simplification. These are specialist judgments, not measured rankings or approval to implement/publish.
