# Professional projection-art benchmarks and original forest directions

Research date: 2026-10-15. Specialist 01. Scope: artist/studio primary sources, visual dramaturgy and transfer to one domestic projector. Latest direction from Shane: **dark enchanted forest and spectral creatures**. This report proposes art; it does not authorize or implement code, acquire assets, publish a release, or claim projector acceptance.

## Main finding

The strongest direction is an inhabited forest that seems to occupy the actual room: recognizable bark and branches, a carefully withheld creature, and one consequential transformation per sequence. The useful professional benchmark is the relationship among place, attention and reveal. A busy supply of Halloween symbols does not supply that relationship.

Make the architecture participate. A doorway can become a narrow clearing, a seam can become a root fissure, and a corner can conceal a creature before it emerges. A projector filling every available surface continuously can weaken all three effects. The proposals below reserve large quiet areas so a small movement has somewhere to register.

## Six benchmarks

### 1. URBANSCREEN — FinsterWald, Grimmwelt Kassel, 2019

**Documented:** the exhibition uses changing, sometimes associative and sometimes concrete letter formations; the forest is conceived as a growing, meandering living being. A central circle of steles contains a video sequence with open and hidden fairy-tale references rather than a literal retelling. The creator describes an experience adaptable to other locations. [Official project page](https://www.urbanscreen.com/finsterwald/) (opened page lines 12–18).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** a forest can appear intelligent through changes in organization, without putting a face on every tree. For one projector, use two or three trunk-like vertical areas to frame a single ritual clearing. Let scattered marks first read as bark, then briefly align into a creature-shaped absence. The viewer recognizes a presence before receiving a complete explanation. A quiet enclosing environment and one charged center offer stronger hierarchy than simultaneous characters everywhere.

**Limit:** the gallery’s actual stele geometry and surrounding environment contribute physical depth; a single flat rectangle will need authored layering and architectural framing. Do not assume text fragments alone will convey “forest” from a residential viewing distance.

### 2. Moment Factory — Forest of Shadows, Seattle and Dallas, 2025

**Documented:** a seasonal woodland attraction combining soundscapes, light design, illusions and projections that distort familiar surroundings. The stated emotional tension is fear versus curiosity; it explicitly avoids gore and shock. It reused Astra Lumina infrastructure with a different narrative and visual identity. [Official project page](https://momentfactory.com/products/forest-of-shadows) (opened page lines 110–117).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** make recognition unreliable rather than increasing spectacle continuously. A shape initially explained as a branch can develop an anatomically impossible second motion. A still clearing can contain a distant watcher; approaching light reveals only its shadow. One projector can support that ambiguity in a modest framed area, provided background detail gives the eye a stable reference.

**Limit:** an outdoor walk obtains suspense from bodily progression, separated stations and directional sound. A short loop must create those stages through visual attention and timing. The page does not document a particular creature or exact transition, so it is not evidence for copying either.

### 3. Moment Factory — Foresta Lumina, Coaticook, 2014

**Documented:** a two-kilometre night route introduces characters derived from local myths and legends. Its custom scenography combines projections and lighting, ethereal sound, perforated manuscript-like metal panels, fairy-like light units, and mapping onto natural elements. [Official project page](https://momentfactory.com/products/foresta-lumina) (opened page lines 110–120).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** small luminous events can belong to an environment instead of floating as decorative overlays. On one projected woodland scene, lights should perch, gather around a hollow, retreat behind branches and trigger a visible response. The environment then supplies a miniature social world. A hierarchy of foreground branches, a middle clearing and a distant origin for the lights provides readable depth without constant camera movement.

**Limit:** do not transfer the installation’s physical scale or imply a projected room replaces a real forest. A domestic version needs one legible episode, not a compressed checklist of trail stations. Tiny all-over lights alone risk becoming a generic particles preset.

### 4. URBANSCREEN — 555 KUBIK, Hamburg

**Documented:** the concept derives from the architecture and visual pattern of the Hamburger Kunsthalle’s Galerie der Gegenwart. Its narrative intention is to dissolve and break through the strict façade, revealing alternative readings of its geometry through graphics and movement. [Official project page](https://www.urbanscreen.com/555-kubik/) (opened page lines 10–18).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** establish a material rule, violate it, then let the viewer reassess the real object. In the forest direction, a wall panel could first appear covered in continuous bark; a seam opens as if the wall has become an immense tree hollow. The useful unit is a convincing local transformation, not an entire house collapsing. Existing mapped surfaces can provide boundaries; authored shading and occlusion must explain what those boundaries become.

**Limit:** this page is concept evidence, not confirmation of any specific hand, folding cube, falling block or beat sometimes associated with circulating videos. Architectural illusion can weaken off-axis; the proposed reveal should retain an attractive composition even when the depth trick is imperfect.

### 5. Skullmapping — Skeleton Dance, Brussels Light Festival, 2013

**Documented:** projection onto cobblestones opens the street to an underworld containing skeletons. The described trajectory starts as nightmare and turns toward a romantic ending. The creator compares the technique to moving street painting. [Official project page](https://skullmapping.com/project/skeleton-dance/) (opened page lines 5–12; directing, animation and sound credits lines 17–22).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** a threatening discovery can become a relationship. A spectral woodland animal need not lunge or grin: it might protect a smaller light, inspect the viewer, or return something to a tree hollow. For a single projector, stage an apparent opening on one known surface, give the character a place to stand, and make one readable choice change its meaning. A before-and-after emotional contrast supplies an ending that particles cannot.

**Limit:** ground anamorphosis and viewing position are a specific staging arrangement, not a universally portable illusion. The exact dance movements, duration and romantic resolution were not watched here and must not be described as observed.

### 6. Limelight + Gabriel Schama — Cathedral of Thieves, 2018

**Documented:** projected animation augments Schama’s intricate layered plywood relief. The work premiered at The Other Art Fair in Santa Monica; the object measures 42 by 62 inches, and its mapped sequence exceeds two and a half minutes. The creator emphasizes tiny details rather than a building-scale show. [Official project page](https://www.limelight.art/portfolio/cathedral-of-thieves-with-gabriel-schama) (opened page lines 9–26).

**Dramatic device and transfer — inference:** craft can come from matching image structure to material structure. In a domestic forest scene, illuminate a coherent root or branch path, let it carry a pulse to a hollow, and allow the creature to emerge from that connected system. The important lesson is precise attachment and continuity, not ornamental complexity for its own sake. A small convincing focal object can feel more intentional than a much larger generic full-frame loop.

**Limit:** the plywood supplies real relief and occlusion. A flat projection cannot claim those properties merely by adding fine linework. Detail must survive the actual projector, throw distance and viewing distance; reduce density before sacrificing the main silhouette.

## What transfers across the six

These are synthesis and design judgments, not measured audience results:

1. **Place before ornament.** Give the setting a readable material and spatial organization before adding spectral events. The entity should appear to inhabit it.
2. **A source and a consequence.** A light comes from somewhere; something notices it; the environment changes. Three causally related actions are more memorable than ten independent effects.
3. **One dominant event.** Peripheral motion can suggest a living world, but it should pause or diminish when the creature becomes the subject.
4. **Withhold the complete creature.** First suggest weight, breath, antlers, feathers, or displaced foliage. Reveal enough anatomy for recognition, then reserve one surprising behavior.
5. **Use the actual surface as a participant.** Map openings and boundaries to the place when available; supply a deliberate framed mode for surfaces without useful architecture.
6. **End with a changed interpretation.** An ominous guardian, a creature returning to bark, or a procession discovering the watcher gives a loop an event. The reset must follow the fiction.

## Three original candidates

The concepts below are original synthesis. They borrow broad artistic mechanisms, not protected characters, footage, compositions or musical cues. Each has a complete 24–28-second arc suitable for review as motion. None is a recommendation to limit Potato Mapper to simple procedural cartoons.

### A. The Hollow That Breathes — 28 seconds

**Premise:** the projected wall is the flank of an ancient tree. A hollow appears to contain deep forest; an enormous spectral owl notices the viewer and quietly seals the passage.

**Composition:** an irregular trunk surrounds a clearing taking roughly the central third. Foreground bark overlaps the opening. The owl occupies the middle depth, separated from distant trees. Keep the broad margins quieter and darker than the hollow. Tall and wide arrangements should recompose the clearing rather than stretch the creature.

| Time | Visible beat | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 s | Bark is nearly still. A shallow expanding pulse travels up one root; dust moves only near a seam. | Establish material and locate attention. |
| 5–10 s | The seam widens into a hollow; overlapping bark edges reveal three depths of forest. One feather catches a moving light inside. | Turn a real surface into a threshold; imply an inhabitant. |
| 10–16 s | A large owl rotates from profile toward the viewer. Its torso remains partly hidden by the near trunk. Eyes reflect briefly, then dim. | Reveal presence without relying on two disembodied glowing dots. |
| 16–22 s | The owl opens one wing across the hollow; luminous dust follows its feather structure and the distant forest briefly draws backward. | Deliver the central visual event with anatomy, weight and depth. |
| 22–28 s | The wing becomes overlapping bark. The owl’s contour disappears into grain; the original root pulse returns to rest. | Give the transformation an ending and a fictional loop reset. |

**Practical art route:** a deliberately lit rendered owl and layered forest can be authored offline as a loop, with a small controlled live light layer if useful. A native real-time version is also possible but must match the motion quality. Mesh mapping should preserve the hollow boundary. Current video/image paths are possibilities, not evidence that this particular scene performs or synchronizes correctly yet.

**Weaknesses:** weak feather motion creates a sliding cutout; a permanently bright oval becomes a television-shaped window; deep murky grays may vanish in room light; exaggerated owl eyes become cute. Review the wing as the hardest shot before authoring a whole forest.

**Why shortlist it:** a strong single focal creature, architectural participation and a clear material-to-creature-to-material transformation. The owl is readable without dialogue or audio.

### B. The Stag and the Borrowed Lantern — 26 seconds

**Premise:** a distant light approaches through a dark wood. It appears to be carried by a tall threatening silhouette, but the spectral stag lowers it gently into a root cradle and vanishes.

**Composition:** asymmetrical clearing, diagonal approach path, foreground branch crossing only part of the stag’s body. Avoid a centered heraldic pose. The lantern is the brightest compact area; antlers are initially read from interrupted reflections rather than a continuous neon outline.

| Time | Visible beat | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 s | A lantern-sized light appears between distant trunks. Near foliage stays still. | Establish distance and curiosity. |
| 5–11 s | Light is occluded by successive trunks; a tall antlered silhouette becomes identifiable through a slow, weighted step. | Make approach spatial and corporeal. |
| 11–17 s | The stag stops off-center, turns an ear and lowers its head toward a root cradle. Its outline separates briefly into drifting bark fragments. | Create uncertainty while preserving recognizable anatomy. |
| 17–22 s | The lantern settles into the cradle; a small ring of seedlings responds. The stag steps backward and becomes forest shadow. | Reframe threat as guardianship through a specific action. |
| 22–26 s | The seedlings fold closed around the light. One distant glint restarts the approach path. | Reset with an environmental action instead of a simple fade. |

**Practical art route:** authored skeletal animation or a high-quality rendered loop supplies anatomy; controlled layers can provide parallax, foreground occlusion and a restrained light response. The AMD 840M target argues for profiling a chosen implementation, not preemptively discarding detailed creature art. Use authored translucent values appropriate to the actual blend path.

**Weaknesses:** foot sliding and antler clipping immediately expose the construction. It needs more creature animation craft than A. A light-only deer outline would lose the central premise of weight and behavior. Sparse branch paths can also resemble holiday decoration if hue and glow dominate material detail.

**Why shortlist it:** the creature makes a legible choice, with atmosphere preceding spectacle. It offers an emotionally richer Halloween mood while retaining unease.

### C. The Procession Behind the Trees — 24 seconds

**Premise:** small spectral creatures cross a moonless clearing behind foreground trees. One notices the viewer; the entire procession stops, extinguishes its lights and leaves an impossible moving shadow behind.

**Composition:** horizontal travel through a narrow visible corridor. Three foreground trunks provide genuine occlusion events; creatures differ in gait and silhouette but share an organic material treatment. The watcher is placed near one third, not always at the center. A tall mode should stage an ascending root stair instead of rotating the wide version.

| Time | Visible beat | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 s | One small lantern crosses between trees. A second follows late enough to establish a procession rather than a particle stream. | Establish scale and expectation. |
| 4–10 s | Three distinct creatures move with offset rhythms: a long-eared walker, a mothlike hovering form and a low root-backed carrier. | Build a strange social world through motion variety. |
| 10–15 s | The long-eared creature stops, turns toward the room and lifts its lantern. Others brake in sequence behind it. | Transfer attention from travel to audience awareness. |
| 15–19 s | Lights close one by one. All bodies vanish into the trunks, but one large shadow keeps moving in the opposite direction. | Break the established physical rule once. |
| 19–24 s | The shadow contracts into a knot of bark. A new distant lantern passes behind it and restores the opening state. | Resolve the anomaly and restart cleanly. |

**Practical art route:** animated creature assets, layered depth masks and one authored shadow pass can create this with a single image plane or several mapped surfaces. The art must be staged for a definite viewing distance. More characters are not automatically better; three distinct actions offer a reasonable first production boundary.

**Weaknesses:** the greatest risk is a side-scrolling sticker parade. Feet, mutual spacing, variable pacing, foreground occlusion and the collective stop must carry performance. Tiny bodies may be unreadable on a large dim wall. Avoid generic bouncing ghosts and repeated uniform bobbing.

**Why shortlist it:** it gives spectral creatures a shared behavior and delivers a disturbing reveal without requiring a monumental creature. It is also the most adaptable to a wide multi-surface composition, though that is an artistic hypothesis requiring a mapped test.

## Comparative judgment and review criteria

| Candidate | Strongest quality | Highest art risk | Best first review shot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Hollow | Surface transformation and one commanding presence | Owl becoming a flat cutout | Wing turning into bark, 16–28 s |
| B. Stag | Creature behavior and emotional reversal | Weight, gait and head/antler continuity | Final approach and lowering lantern, 8–20 s |
| C. Procession | Ensemble attention and eerie collective response | Sticker-parade motion and small scale | Stop, look and shadow contradiction, 10–19 s |

My order is **A first, B second, C third**. This is a design recommendation, not an audience score or a substitute for Shane’s judgment. A should advance only if its creature and transformation look convincing in moving footage. If the core shot fails, adding more particle effects will not rescue it.

For the eventual five-concept shortlist, retain ideas from other specialists that add a genuinely different dramatic device: for example a forest spirit revealed by its effect on its surroundings, or a material transformation with no animal at all. Do not fill all five places with the same glowing-animal reveal under new names.

Before implementation is called successful, the selected scene should demonstrate: a readable full-motion arc at expected viewing distance; intended focal hierarchy; convincing creature mechanics; stable mapped attachment; a loop reset justified by the fiction; and performance on the actual target laptop. Physical projector evidence remains a separate gate. This report provides none of those execution results.
