## Verdict and scoring discipline

The direction is substantially better suited to dark enchanted forest/spectral creatures than the old native visuals. **Neither concept board is a 9.5/10 result.** V2 is a useful revision, not a finished effect, and certainly not evidence of excellent animation. Its most important improvement is separation of forms and fairer comparison, not additional detail.

Scale: 0–2 fundamentally fails the intended experience; 3–4 rudimentary/incoherent; 5–6 serviceable with conspicuous weaknesses; 7 coherent and compelling with substantial revisions remaining; 8 strong work with identifiable weaknesses; 9 exceptional control and specificity; 9.5 exemplary, unusually complete work with only minor weaknesses. These are critical judgments, not measured audience results. Decimal scores express a ranking, not scientific precision.

## Actual image criticism

**Old native art:** the square pumpkin panel has one large and two small, essentially equivalent grinning symbols. Wide/tall variants mostly change their available empty space. Ghosts have round heads, short side arms, simple friendly expressions and scalloped hems. Their white fill gives strong immediate recognition, but no tension, environmental integration or convincing material. This is not a demand for more polygons: their emotional premise and composition are wrong for this brief. The rain has neither a focal incident nor a consequential interaction with the window. Functional shape coverage must not be mistaken for visual staging.

**Crown:** V1's huge trunk frame, torso void and bent neck give it the best creature/environment connection. V2 moves the shoulder and jaw onto lighter fog and makes the structure easier to parse. Yet neither frame demonstrates a tree becoming a creature: both depict an already completed monster. The many fine antler tips still mingle with forest twigs, the fibers produce a ragged beard, and the glowing eye remains a shortcut. Remove the eye and retain a legible jaw/neck gap, torso void and three dominant fork gestures. Preserve those landmarks across a turn before adding surface detail. A beautiful monster painting with drifting fog would fail the intended transformation.

**Procession:** V2's three people are much clearer and the ground has a believable light path. This is the strongest correction between boards. The fixed lantern hanging on a chain at upper right is not evidence of an absent carrier; it looks like a mounted prop. The foreground robe still needs a distinct load-bearing pose, not just a larger duplicate. A held frame must establish hand, lantern and foot relationships; the moving proof then breaks exactly one relationship. Keep the missing-carrier incident and remove the extra wrong-shadow subplot.

**Moon:** large crows and moon provide the clearest immediate silhouette contrast. V2 does not obey its own simplification request: hundreds of tiny birds remain. The board depicts a flock over a moon, not the proposed impossible wing or leaf transformation. Its polish hides how familiar the premise is. It is a strong candidate for broad spectacle but needs a better event than a particle cloud tracing a curve.

**Oracle:** V1's reptilian slit pupil almost entirely defines the entity. V2 removes that cliché and has a convincing heavy upper wood plate over an amber cavity. This is a useful shift, but the cavity can now read as a lit crack or small furnace. The stated three-path causal network is not actually visible; large foreground roots and mushrooms replace it. Show a before/arrival/opening sequence in which the moving tension explains awareness without needing an eyeball.

**Wall:** V2 removes the repeated stag and offers clearer forest depth. Nevertheless it is still a broken plaster picture frame around a woodland illustration. The right-hand figure looks like another cloaked traveler; it does not establish a presence materially different from Procession. No still can prove attached peeling, but an angled hinge edge and consistent thickness could at least support the proposed mechanics. The current border texture cannot substitute for that evidence. This is the weakest selection when the goal is a diverse creature collection.

## Five-slot selection and potential, not completed-work scores

My choice is **01 Crown, 02 Procession, 04 Oracle, 17 Weaver, 19 Returning Roost**, with 03 Moon the first alternate. This agrees with report 10 for specific reasons below; it is not approval of unseen art for 17/19. Their design descriptions are the only evidence available. The five must earn different physical rules, not merely different silhouettes in the same gray forest.

The potential scores below estimate artistic promise under a good execution. They are neither predicted finished scores nor claims that the project already reaches them.

| Selected treatment | Potential / risk | Reason to keep and specific condition |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Hollow Crown | **8.8/10 potential; very high deformation risk** | Strong protagonist and recognition reversal. Keep torso void, neck bend and dominant crown fork stable through tree/creature correspondence. Do not require full locomotion. If the morph stretches like rubber, stop detailing and revise the structure. |
| 02 Lantern Procession | **8.5/10 potential; high performance/staging risk** | Collective repetition followed by a missing carrier creates a different kind of unease. Three distinct gaits and grounded lantern weight are essential. Use one contradiction; the additional wrong shadow weakens focus. |
| 04 Mycelium Oracle | **8.6/10 potential; high identity risk** | Macro material behavior changes scale and pace. Keep fungal patches distinct from thicker wood/root structures. A pulse arriving, material opening and directed response must be readable; otherwise it is a glowing crack. |
| 17 Weaver Above | **8.2/10 potential; high projection and rigging risk** | Overhead jointed movement and a suspended mass diversify the body language. Do not make silk/dew the subject: use a large partly concealed abdomen, a few decisive leg/contact silhouettes, trunk deflection and broad shadows. A generic large spider is only a familiar monster; its interaction with the canopy must create the event. No static art supplied, so no static-art score. |
| 19 Returning Roost | **8.4/10 potential; high causality risk** | A few large birds and impossible missing supports offer a more specific authored breach than a dense eclipse flock. The missing support must be a large unmistakable silhouette interruption after a clearly grounded landing. A subtle twig disappearing will look like an error. No static art supplied, so no static-art score. |

**Why 19 over 03:** 03 wins immediate scale/spectacle and has actual attractive concept pixels. 19 wins specificity and a bounded small cast, while demanding convincing landings and a carefully taught support rule. The lead should choose 19 for the intended authored collection, with a strict escape hatch: if a silent low-detail storyboard cannot communicate branch loss without explanatory text, restore 03 and build a clean broad eclipse rather than more bird particles. Do not combine both crow concepts into one overloaded scene. This is a narrow choice under uncertainty, not an established audience preference.

**Why 17 over 05:** 05 is valuable to projection mapping but its panel/viewpoint dependence and second hidden forest presence duplicate more of Crown/Procession. 17 adds jointed vertical/overhead locomotion and suspended weight. It is not inherently cheaper: convincing leg placements, body clearance, occlusion and shadows are substantial work. Reject the substitution if the actual design is mostly thin web line art with a small spider. For a future architecture-specific installation, 05 could beat 17; that is a different production emphasis.

**Challenge to the selector:** report 10 responsibly admits overlapping uncertainty, the missing rival art and 18's slightly higher raw score than 17. Keep that caution in the final recommendation; its weighted decimals must not create a false impression of resolved ranking. Candidate18, Shed That Walks, is a credible replacement for Crown: the empty interior and three stiff supports could achieve a stranger material identity with less reliance on antlered-skull familiarity. Keeping Crown is a choice of the better-developed first proof, not a finding that it is more original or cheaper. If its correspondence fails, compare18 before spending further detail effort. Conversely, a giant spider does not earn novelty merely by having more articulated legs. The one measured advance and delayed canopy tension must carry its selection.

The portfolio still risks sameness: 01, 02 and 17 cannot all be a looming brown body slowly emerging through fog. Assign distinct scales and energy: Crown's single recognition/turn, Procession's grounded lateral route, Oracle's close material contraction, Weaver's overhead supported movement, and Roost's discrete landing/absence/departure. Reserve antlers for Crown and direct eye contact for at most one encounter. Keep sky/negative space in Roost so replacing Moon does not eliminate every open composition.

## Production realism

The concept images contain neither a rig nor persistent geometry. They do not demonstrate that the same antler can be held coherent across views, that a robe can walk, or that the missing branch can be reconstructed. An offline-authored composite is a sensible route to test because it can carry richer original animation without first building a new creature engine inside the mapper; it still requires designed assets, actual animation, rendering, storage and observed playback. The inspected research's recommendation is not a finished asset pipeline. Nor should the old native icon effects be extrapolated into a promise that a few shader additions reproduce this imagery. Native fog and support motion can be useful only after they serve a proved act. No new capability, frame rate, seamless decoding or all-surface compatibility is established here.

## Requested revision before a final production judgment

I requested a new board before judging V2. V2 answers the equal-size comparison, repeated antler and literal-eye objections, but only partly answers simplification. **Do not spend another pass polishing a five-scene mood sheet.** The useful next research revision is an equal-scale three-keyframe strip for each selected treatment: normal rule, one supernatural breach, recovery/concealment. Use large flat value groups, no microtexture and no explanatory text inside the frames. Add 17 and 19, which remain unpictured. For Crown hide eyes; Procession show the same lantern with and without the hand; Oracle show the arriving channel and plate motion; Weaver mark actual supports; Roost show an unmistakably missing branch span.

Those strips can test whether the concepts communicate at all. They still do not establish animation. A later authorized moving proof must show stable anatomy/material, purposeful holds, consequential secondary action and at least three complete joins. Whole-image fog, independent random motion, glow pulses and global fade resets are not substitutes for the act. Judge the actual output at the milestone it reaches. The present research has earned a serious next proof, not a 9.5 celebration or implementation/publication authorization.
