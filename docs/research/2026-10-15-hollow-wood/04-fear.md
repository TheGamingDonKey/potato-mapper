# Specialist 04 — fear perception, uncanny motion and reveal timing

15 October 2026. Research and proposed acceptance tasks only. Read the shared brief and all twenty hypotheses. No animation was rendered or watched, no audience test was run, and no projector was observed. This report does not award a finished-work score.

## Decision

Keep 01 as the first moving proof, but its decisive event must reveal **a large agent noticing the viewer**, not simply assemble an attractive deer. Keep 02 as the strongest alternative because a readable procession can establish an ordinary motion rule before breaking it once. 03 is presently an attention spectacle; 04 is presently a material/lighting spectacle; 05 is presently an architectural reveal. Each needs observable agency or a consequential interruption to earn the requested ominous identity. More glow, facial detail or particle count does not establish that identity.

The research supports separating three outcomes: an event can attract attention without frightening anyone; a mismatch can change action-processing responses without proving felt uncanniness; a frightening experience need not be the most enjoyable. Treat the following art choices as hypotheses to compare in moving images.

## Four primary studies and their actual limits

### 1. Motion and looming can capture attention; random agitation is not equivalent

[Franconeri & Simons (2003), *Moving and looming stimuli capture attention* — author-hosted paper](https://visionplus.psych.northwestern.edu/Papers_files/FranconeriSimons_Motion.pdf).

The study used letter-search tasks, not horror viewing. Experiment 3 reported 37 Harvard undergraduates in analyzed looming/receding conditions, with four additional participants excluded for high error rates. Expanding items received search priority; contracting items did not show the same capture. Experiment 2 included 52 participants across motion conditions; translation/disocclusion and rapid jitter did not produce interchangeable results. The authors' behavioral-urgency account is an interpretation, not a direct demonstration of fear. Generalization is limited by laboratory tasks, simplified displays and student samples.

**Applicable:** do not assume every moving leaf attracts useful attention. **Design transfer:** reserve a single forward advance or expansion for a meaningful event; compare it with a matched retreat. Neither looming nor a startle should be described as a guaranteed scare.

### 2. Appearance–movement mismatch is a useful lead, not a universal uncanny recipe

[Saygin et al. (2012), *The thing that should not be* — author-lab PDF](https://sayginlab.ucsd.edu/files/2013/01/SayginEtAl_2012_SCAN.pdf); [author publication listing](https://sayginlab.ucsd.edu/publications/).

Twenty adults aged 20–36 participated; one participant's imaging data were unusable because of head movement. Human, mechanical robot and human-looking android action clips were compared using fMRI repetition suppression. Stronger effects for the android in parts of the action-perception system were interpreted as consistent with appearance–motion prediction error. This does not establish that any deliberately bad animation is frightening, or that a woody deer behaves like a human-looking android. **Access limitation:** the author PDF's indexed methods excerpt and original abstract were accessible, but full-PDF opens failed; do not treat this as a full methods audit.

**Applicable:** form and movement should be designed together. **Design transfer:** establish a plausible motion rule before a controlled violation. A floating body with sliding feet throughout may just read as an animation defect.

### 3. Threat anticipation can focus attention, but the studied suspense had narrative context

[Bezdek et al. (2015), *Neural evidence that suspense narrows attentional focus* — author-hosted full paper](https://control.gatech.edu/wp-content/uploads/pubs/Bezdek-et-al-2015-Neuroscience.pdf).

Nineteen undergraduates participated; 18 contributed usable fMRI data. They watched ten film excerpts with central fixation and peripheral checkerboards; introductory text supplied narrative context. Suspense time courses came from a separate 23-person rating sample. Rising suspense was associated with increased central and reduced peripheral visual-region activity. The films concerned possible negative outcomes for characters. Eye movements were not measured. This is not evidence that a silent, context-free 24-second wall loop will produce the same attentional pattern, or that spectators cannot notice peripheral events.

**Applicable:** a potential outcome matters, not merely dark scenery. **Design transfer:** give viewers one unresolved question—will that presence turn toward us?—and keep the answer in the established focal region. Do not hide the only important reveal at an unrelated edge while a bright center continues competing.

### 4. Fear and enjoyment should be measured separately

[Andersen et al. (2020), *Playing With Fear: A Field Study in Recreational Horror* — original publisher full text](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797620972116).

110 visitors aged 12–57 entered a Danish haunted attraction; 92 remained after exclusions. Across ratings of three locations, fear and enjoyment showed an inverted-U relationship. The overall attraction ratings did not show the same relationship. This was one evening at a live attraction with actors, groups and sound; it cannot supply an optimal fear level or timing formula for domestic projection. Self-reported enjoyment also did not reliably agree with observers' enjoyment judgments.

**Applicable:** an independent critic's visible-reaction reading cannot substitute for asking viewers. **Design transfer:** seek repeatable ominous enjoyment, not maximum intensity, and record eerie/threatening, enjoyable and cute as separate responses. Do not infer a preference from a smile.

## Challenge the five candidates

The following are original art-direction judgments, not findings from those experiments.

| Candidate | Likely route to a pretty or cute result | Required change in the moving proof | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Hollow Crown** | Symmetric glowing antlers, large round eyes, soft head bob and a clean deer outline create a friendly mascot. Assembly alone is a logo reveal. | Make the figure materially continuous with large crooked trunks. Its turn should reclassify previously still forest structure as a body. Use a heavy, readable pivot and a short settled gaze; keep the face largely occluded and avoid expressive eyebrows/mouth. | **02 Lantern Procession** | Equally spaced bobbing lights and identical little travelers become a parade. Amber becomes cozy if every light constantly bathes the scene. | Establish two or three partial carriers behind trunks with different silhouettes, coherent path and visible occlusion. Stop one human-scale motion rule once: the final lantern continues while its carrier remains behind. Keep the discrepancy readable in the same area. | The absent carrier is explained by ordinary darkness or viewers see a line of decorative floating orbs. |
| **03 Murder of the Moon** | Decorative spirals, all-over sparkles and a perfect wing resemble a screensaver or fantasy title card. | Preserve flock travel direction and wingbeat grouping before the mass briefly blocks the moon. Let the revealed giant wing interrupt that route, occlude a foreground branch and depart with apparent weight. Avoid a bird-shaped particle logo held in empty space. | Viewers report only a vortex, leaves or a shape morph; the flock-to-agent change cannot be retold. This remains the weakest of the five for dread without a stronger beat. |
| **04 Mycelium Oracle** | Uniform emissive tubes, a symmetric face and an eye blink produce science-fiction circuitry or a smiling tree. | Use irregular buried propagation, selective surface illumination and a strongly asymmetric bark cavity. The one eye-like opening should interrupt the incoming wave, hold briefly, then darken the surrounding network as if taking ownership of it. Avoid cute eyelid gestures and synchronized pulsing everywhere. | The wave remains the perceived subject and the face reads as a friendly ornament or is not seen at projection distance. |
| **05 Forest Behind the Wall** | A round glowing opening with a beautiful distant forest becomes a fantasy portal. | Keep the opening irregular, materially rooted and fixed to the composed surface. Establish deep still trunks, then let one apparently distant vertical form shift independently and partly cross behind the near seam. The event should imply a watcher occupying depth, not a camera flying through a portal. | Depth depends on camera travel, or viewers only describe an attractive window/crack with no presence. Arbitrary room geometry still needs separate mapping validation. |

Do not merge all five into one escalating montage. That would erase the controlled comparisons and produce competing reveal mechanisms. A face, eye, giant wing and root portal together are not automatically more frightening.

## Two revised beats for an initial comparison

These durations and proportions are starting design values, not research-derived thresholds. Both are silent, fixed-camera, single-composition proposals that can be baked into one looping video; they assume no shared QMediaPlayer transport or audio changes.

### A. Hollow Crown — the grove notices you (24 seconds)

**0–6 s:** a readable layered grove; slow breeze affects minor branches while a large central fork remains still. Leave enough low-level motion to establish the environmental rule without moving every pixel. **6–10 s:** a restrained side-light pass connects the fork, an occluded neck and antlers; the viewer can begin to recognize a huge being. **10–15 s:** the neck turns across the light while the main trunks remain planted. The turn carries a previously stationary branch cluster with it, converting a piece of environment into anatomy. **15–18 s:** settle into a near-frontal hold, with no friendly nod, smile, eye enlargement or full-screen flash. **18–24 s:** a near bough occludes the head as its structure becomes ordinary branches again; return light and breeze to the initial state behind that occlusion.

Compare a control with the same geometry, light and duration but no purposeful turn. If both are only attractive forest decoration, adding texture will not solve the missing event. If the turn looks broken, correct its joint/path construction before interpreting it as uncanny.

### B. Lantern Procession — the carrier stays behind (24 seconds)

**0–7 s:** two low lights travel on one path behind large trunks; brief passing illumination establishes tall partial carriers and human-scale carrying height. **7–12 s:** the final carrier reaches one trunk and stops; the lantern briefly rests too. **12–16 s:** the lantern resumes its established trajectory alone while the carrier's narrow silhouette remains visibly stationed at the trunk. Keep both forms readable at once; do not rely on viewers remembering a tiny figure from seconds earlier. **16–19 s:** the stationary silhouette turns slightly toward the continuing light, then the light passes behind a foreground trunk. **19–24 s:** a moving branch occludes the waiting silhouette and the path returns to the start condition. Use the same branch/lighting state at the boundary.

Compare with an ordinary carrier-and-light continuation. Keep the impossible separation as the sole rule violation. A new face jumping out here would prevent us from learning whether the quiet discrepancy worked.

## Measurable acceptance tasks before calling anything scary

These are proposed small production gates, not validated psychometric tests or population claims. No results exist yet.

1. **Silent recognition:** show each moving proof once without its title to five willing viewers unfamiliar with the concept. Ask for one sentence describing the event before offering labels. At least four should independently identify both the forest setting and the intended agency change. Record exact counts and ambiguous descriptions; a five-person pilot cannot validate general appeal.
2. **Anticipation, not retrospect alone:** in a separate viewing, pause just before the decisive act and ask what they expect to change next. Compare with a version that omits the setup. If the intended location/agent is no clearer with setup, the anticipation is unproven. Do not demand one exact predicted outcome: unresolved alternatives can be useful.
3. **Cute and threatening are separate:** after each untitled loop, collect independent 0–10 ratings for eerie/threatening, enjoyment, visual clarity and cute/friendly. Compare A/B versions in counterbalanced order and preserve individual values. Suggested local gate: ominous version preferred by at least four of five for eerie/threatening without reducing median clarity; discuss failures instead of averaging them away. The numerical gate is a production choice, not a finding of the cited studies.
4. **Distance and black-level proxy:** repeat recognition at quarter-size and with a specified lifted-black preview, recording the exact transform. Gate on recognition of the same agency change, not tiny eyes or wisps. This is a readability proxy; only the actual projector, room light and viewing position can establish physical visibility.
5. **Loop habituation and seam:** watch three consecutive cycles silently. Ask after cycle three whether the action still holds interest and where the restart occurred. Log unintended cuts/pops and any fall in interest. A repeated loop will become predictable; no guarantee of continuing surprise is credible. Its material transformation and rhythm must also reward anticipation.
6. **Motion audit:** step through the pivot or separation with visible occluder layers and compare the control at matching times. Flag sliding contact, intersecting anatomy, inconsistent carrier/light registration and unintended whole-image scaling. These defects can resemble intentional wrongness in a still but fail in motion.

The narrow milestone is a legible, ominous 24-second event that survives silent playback and a basic distance/black-level challenge. Research does not replace that evidence. Native GL3.3 and video feasibility remain separate engineering decisions, and physical projection remains a separate acceptance step.
