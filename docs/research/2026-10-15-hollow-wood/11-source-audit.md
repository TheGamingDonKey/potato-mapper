# 11 — Independent source and research audit

15 October 2026. Specialist 11. Research-method audit of reports 01–09, `00-brief-and-candidates.md`, `CONCEPTS.md`, `source-links.csv`, and the coordinator's separately supplied `00-visual-evidence.md`. Read all nine reports in full. Inspected the actual concept board. No product code, media production, installation, benchmark, audience experiment, physical projection or publication was performed.

## Verdict

**A useful, generally honest research foundation, with incomplete direct motion analysis. It does not establish a 9.5/10 result or prove the shortlist's quality.** The sampled primary sources mostly support the limited facts attributed to them. The reports usually mark artistic transfers, timing choices, numerical production gates and performance estimates as hypotheses. That is legitimate research use: a practitioner or paper can inspire an experiment without proving that viewers will feel dread.

The most consequential methodological weakness is that specialists 01, 02, 03 and 08 explicitly did not watch their film references. Their motion advice is therefore a synthesis of written process descriptions and proposed staging. The coordinator's visual log records selected frames from an official 38-second Forest of Shadows promo, and explicitly avoids claiming continuous frame-by-frame observation. That is useful visual evidence, but it does not establish a material transformation, motion continuity, readable hold or reset mechanism in a reference film. Do not quietly promote either kind of evidence to an observed motion benchmark.

Two corrections are concrete: 06 needs an explicit manual-unmute qualification; the inspected source manifest omitted all eight source URLs used by 08 and 09. Root has indicated these will be handled as logged errata/manifest maintenance rather than silently rewriting the archived specialist reports. This audit records the versions inspected, not an assertion that those repairs have already happened.

## Method and limits of this audit

I independently reopened the primary pages/papers below, read the relevant passages, and compared their scope with the report claims. This is claim sampling rather than a certification of every sentence or a replication of any experiment. Successful current access cannot establish exactly what a previous agent saw. Conversely, a current failed URL does not prove a previous access claim was fabricated.

The audit used browser text retrieval for primary material and inspected local source where app behavior mattered. The Blender animation page failed through the web wrapper, but a separate direct `Invoke-WebRequest` succeeded; its returned title identified Blender 5.2 LTS and its body contained the documented recoverable frame-sequence workflow. Saygin's lab PDF failed directly; the original open-access article was successfully opened at PMC instead. No reference film was played by this auditor. The visual-board judgments below come from viewing the actual PNG, not from its filename or a generated description.

## Local app facts and required correction

I independently inspected `src/model.cpp`, `src/main.cpp`, `src/canvas.cpp` and `src/patterns.h` for the sampled behavior. This is source evidence, not a live audio/playback test.

- **Audio:** `model.cpp:36` initializes `QAudioOutput` muted; `main.cpp:154–155` supplies a checked Mute audio checkbox whose toggle calls `setMuted(v)`. `main.cpp:387` reads the source's mute state. 06's sentence about sound being outside its proof can be valid as a chosen silent deliverable, but omitting manual unmuting invites an erroneous “app has no audio” conclusion. Required erratum: **“Video audio is muted by default and can be enabled with Mute audio; this first proof is deliberately silent. Speaker playback and audio persistence were not tested.”** 00, CONCEPTS and 08 already contain the essential qualification in the inspected versions.
- **Shared players:** `Scene::source` at `model.cpp:260–264` caches by the exact path string. Same-path surfaces reuse a player. Distinct files do not gain a common start/seek clock from that cache. “All surfaces have independent players” would be wrong; 06's qualification correctly avoids that claim. The cache also means mute/play/seek actions concern the reused source, not necessarily an isolated surface. No live interaction test was performed.
- **Silhouettes/compositing:** `canvas.cpp:161–162` confirms Screen and Add blend factors. For nonnegative color values neither darkens a brighter destination. A dark hero must be composed with its lit background or use a verified Normal/masking route. This is a valid implementation constraint, distinct from ambient light on the wall.
- **Native depth and filtering:** depth testing is disabled in the mapping pass (`canvas.cpp:145`); media uses linear minification (`:169,177`). These observations support 05's caution about a new scene renderer and unresolved fine detail, not a claim that GL3.3 cannot render rich work.
- **Native timing:** `patterns.h:14` supplies 12-second periods only for IDs14–18; other patterns use 10,000 seconds, and phase wrapping occurs at `canvas.cpp:208–209`. A proposed 24-second native narrative needs deliberate timing work. This was correctly identified rather than claimed implemented.

## Coverage, repetition and evidence quality

**Coverage exists across all nine remits.** Professional projection, cinematography, procedural motion, perception research, renderer constraints, offline production, physical readability, choreography/sound, and biological/material constraints each have substantive treatment. 04, 05, 07, 08 and 09 also challenge specific weaknesses rather than merely congratulate the root concept.

**Coverage is not independence.** Several later specialists read the same brief, shortlist and board. Their preference for Crown is correlated and informed by the same early selection and unusually large hero image. It must not be counted as nine blind endorsements. 07 correctly exposes the board-size bias, and 09 correctly finds repeated antlers, eyes and root material. Direct board inspection supports those concerns: the procession leader has an antler motif, the wall reuses an antlered presence, the Oracle has an overt reptilian eye, and the crowded flock competes with its large crow. These are art-direction findings, not factual source errors.

**Many recommendations repeat.** “One dominant event,” broad silhouette, three depths, occlusion, restrained glow, single composite video, and projector checks occur throughout. Some agreement is justified by real constraints. Repetition alone does not add evidence. A final synthesis should retain each repeated rule once, then preserve the genuinely different contributions: 04's matched controls and separate emotional measures; 05's actual shader/blend/timing hazards; 06's three distinct seams; 07's equal-size composition comparison; 08's reset-coverage and timing contradictions; 09's material/silhouette exclusions.

**Numbers are authored, not invented findings.** The inspected reports explicitly identify 24 seconds, luminance ranges, 45–75% creature height, 3–5 fog sheets, CRF/GOP starting values, 0.7-second lead, and five-person/four-of-five gates as proposals or estimates. No sampled report presents them as measured optimums. Do not turn them into requirements “proved by research” during synthesis. A five-person test is useful formative feedback, not a statistical validation of general appeal.

**Access is mostly candid.** 01's absent film viewing, 03's abstract-only Neyret access, 04's failed Saygin full PDF, 06's direct-HTML fallback, 07's rejected Extron access and 09's abstract-only wood chapter are disclosed. I found no sampled false full-access claim. I have not audited every original tool log, so this is not a blanket provenance certification.

**Source manifest gap:** the version inspected listed no 08 or 09 rows. Add their four practitioner and four institutional source URLs, retain report attribution, and add the working Saygin primary alternate above as an audit/errata reference. Link count is a navigation aid, not a quality score.

## Best next research gap: one motion transfer, observed precisely

Use the **official Transfiguration (2024) film linked on the artist's project page** to inspect one complete material transition. Record actual timestamped before/during/after frames and observe the intervening movement. Track head/torso/foot trajectories, silhouette landmarks, opacity and self-occlusion. Do not infer the transition from the descriptive paragraph.

**Precise question:** Does the entity remain recognizable primarily because its geometric landmarks stay stable, because the continuous gait supplies identity despite silhouette disruption, or both—and which part can transfer to a largely stationary tree becoming a stag?

This tests a consequential assumption behind Crown rather than collecting more mood references. A result may weaken the current transfer: the film may preserve identity chiefly through walking while our treatment removes that cue. If so, retain “persistent landmarks” as our authored hypothesis and prove it separately in a low-detail correspondence animatic. Do not retrofit an observed film technique that the footage does not show. One observed transition can inform a design mechanism; it still cannot establish general fear, a seamless loop, home-projector readability or laptop performance.

The present research supports a provisional comparative moving proof after the requested review gate. It does not support calling the current board an animation, the shortlist audience-validated, or the delivery route tested.
