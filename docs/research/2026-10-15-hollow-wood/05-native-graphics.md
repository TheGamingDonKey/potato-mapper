# 05 — Native graphics feasibility: forest depth, creatures and projection

15 October 2026. Research only. No application code, builds, benchmarks, installations or publications performed. Scope: the five hero candidates in `00-brief-and-candidates.md`, current native renderer, and five primary technical sources. All cost comparisons below are engineering estimates, not measured Radeon 840M performance.

## Recommendation

Use an authored, layered forest scene with controlled occlusion as the native baseline; use a rendered creature/scene where real three-dimensional turning or material transformation is essential. Native C++/GL3.3 is capable of rich work. A richer shader made from more ellipses, glowing outlines and random particles would still risk producing an icon collection. Anatomy, negative space, lighting and animation staging need authored decisions before complexity is added.

For the first Hollow Crown proof, a single composed 24-second video is the least invasive way to judge the actual promise: grove becomes creature, a convincing head turn, then antlers become roots. Compare that with a deliberately scoped native 2.5D version using the same storyboard. A successful native comparison requires changing overlap, silhouette and lighting during the turn; stretching a frontal deer shape is insufficient. Neither route currently has a rendered proof in this report.

## What the inspected code actually supplies

- `src/canvas.cpp:28-43` initializes OpenGL 3.3 and loads the shared GLSL 330 fragment shader. Its vertex input is position plus UV (`:32-37`). Surfaces are warped triangles with UVs (`:225-228`), not a three-dimensional forest mesh. The native pass disables depth testing (`:145`). Consequently, forest depth must initially be authored through ordered layers/masks; this pass does not supply a scene depth buffer or shadow map for volumetric lighting.
- `assets/patterns.frag:318-324` establishes surface-local metric coordinates and angle. Existing seasonal work is procedural 2D compositing: ghosts have sheet caps, hems, arms, eyes and smiles (`:229-266`); their procession is a closed oval with 2–4 copies (`:358-374`). These are implementation observations, not a claim that the technique cannot support better shapes.
- Seasonal scenes output opaque composed colour (`assets/patterns.frag:356,374,403`). The app supports per-surface Normal/Screen/Add (`src/canvas.cpp:158-164`), brightness/opacity and density/flow/angle/palette/edge settings (`:209-211`). Average corner dimensions determine `surfaceAspect` (`:213-216`); this cannot express every local distortion in a heavily deformed mesh.
- Authored media is already sampled (`assets/patterns.frag:569-572`), including NV12 conversion. Loaded media uses linear minification rather than mipmapped minification (`src/canvas.cpp:169,177,200`). A reduced or foreshortened video surface therefore needs particular scrutiny for fine detail; bilinear filtering is not a complete minification filter.
- Dynamic FX is a separate coloured-geometry path (`src/canvas.cpp:45-55,85-99`), not an existing textured creature animation system. Reusing it for skeletal textured cutouts would be new work.

## A genuinely richer bounded native approach

**Environment:** a fixed camera, three clearly separated tree masses, several broad fog strata and a foreground root/canopy mask. Use deliberately designed trunk silhouettes and asymmetrical gaps. Motion should travel through the scene: distant wind first, then the subject responds, then a nearby branch crosses the camera to reset the loop. Avoid synchronous whole-image breathing.

**Fog:** start with 3–5 ordered, low-frequency animated density sheets, each with a capped number of noise octaves and different drift. Composite the sheets between the environment layers so a trunk can obscure one fog sheet and be veiled by another. Define local light falloff around lanterns; do not brighten every fog pixel equally. This is an art-directed depth approximation, not physical participating-media simulation. Its work scales with covered pixels, sheet count and noise complexity, without a per-pixel ray loop.

A true light-shaft ray march would additionally need scene depth and shadow visibility data. Demofox's author tutorial explicitly samples down the ray to scene depth, consulting a shadow map; reducing samples creates banding, while blue-noise jitter exchanges structured bands for noise. That is a useful optional study after the layered proof. It is not a drop-in improvement for the current pass, and a jittered effect without temporal reconstruction can still shimmer. [Ray Marching Fog With Blue Noise](https://blog.demofox.org/2020/05/10/ray-marching-fog-with-blue-noise/)

**Creatures:** use authored filled contours with uneven mass, coherent joints, head/neck separation and intentional occlusion. Bézier contours or triangulated cutouts can carry expressive silhouettes; a small set of bones can move them. Tree branches can share the antler contour language without becoming a uniform fractal. For a meaningful yaw, use a real 3D model rendered offline, or authored pose transitions with changing front/back order. Native 3D rendering is possible within GL3.3, but would be an additional scene renderer, not the existing mapping mesh.

Loop and Blinn's vector-art technique establishes a way to render Bézier boundaries with perspective-aware screen-space antialiasing. It does not provide a creature design or rig. Its internal curve boundaries and triangle edges have different antialiasing requirements; simply enabling MSAA does not solve every procedural boundary. [Rendering Vector Art on the GPU](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-25-rendering-vector-art-gpu)

**Cost control:** rasterize authored branches/cutouts into their bounds or use prefiltered masks rather than evaluate every forest branch SDF at every output pixel. Avoid full-scene sphere tracing plus multiple shadow marches as the first production method. A half-resolution fog buffer could reduce shading load but adds render targets, compositing and edge-upsample design; none was established in the inspected path. Multiple overlapping surfaces and simultaneous preview/output multiply rendered work. Baked media trades shader arithmetic for decode/upload bandwidth and storage; it is not automatically faster on this machine.

## Controls, compositing and shader traps

Preserve IDs 0–18 and append any new effects. The list order is the persisted ID (`src/patterns.h:4-6`); project loading clamps to its current size (`src/model.cpp:131`). Saved size/speed/play and FX settings are explicit (`:125-134`). Existing time can run backwards (`:151`), so native motion should remain a deterministic function of phase, including loop reset. For a 24-second effect, the current period helper needs deliberate extension: only IDs 14–18 get 12 seconds, while all others get 10,000 (`src/patterns.h:14`), and C++ wraps phase before upload (`src/canvas.cpp:208-209`). Do not assume generic defaults fit a narrative scene: current new-pattern flow reset only handles 16–18 (`src/model.cpp:321`).

Recommended control semantics: Density adjusts secondary vegetation/motes, never duplicates the hero; Flow adjusts wind/cloth/fog motion without deleting the main event; Scene size preserves enough environment for context; Speed changes the whole native timeline; Reverse remains deterministic. Angle can rotate the composition and therefore needs an upright default. Media playback is not established as equivalent to these procedural controls: `assignMedia` sets pattern 0 (`src/model.cpp:287`). A video-based scene should honestly retain the media controls it actually has rather than promise parity.

The palette path is a concrete compatibility hazard: only IDs 14–18 get luminance-preserving recolouring (`assets/patterns.frag:54-56`). New IDs with a non-original palette would receive a constant tint, destroying much of their authored shading. Plan explicit scene classification or equivalent treatment before presenting palette support.

Normal compositing can place a dark trunk in front of a bright layer. Screen computes `S + D(1-S)` after the shader weights source colour by alpha (`assets/patterns.frag:59-62`; blend factors `src/canvas.cpp:161`). Add computes alpha-weighted source plus destination (`:162`). For nonnegative colours, neither mode can darken the layer beneath; a black silhouette becomes ineffective over existing bright content. Compose the forest/creature occlusion internally or use Normal for those layers; reserve Screen/Add for emitted light. Repeated glow will lift blacks and flatten depth. This framebuffer behavior cannot remove ambient light from a wall.

Keep derivative work outside fragment-varying culls/discards. `shapeMask` uses `fwidth` (`assets/patterns.frag:25`); the rain implementation already derives footprint from continuous coordinates instead of discontinuous cell identities (`:274-298`). Khronos specifies derivatives and implicit texture derivatives as undefined within non-uniform control flow, and `smoothstep` requires increasing edges. Use `1-smoothstep(low,high,x)`, not reversed edges. Uniform effect/count branches are a different case. [GLSL 3.30 specification, sections 8.3, 8.7, 8.8](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/gl/GLSLangSpec.3.30.pdf)

## Projection readability and evidence gate

Nyquist is a lower sampling bound, not a guarantee that a two-pixel antler or eye will remain readable. Prefilter or remove fine bark/noise octaves when their footprint becomes too small; broad bright-dark relationships should carry the creature. Filtering literature explains why undersampled detail aliases and why bilinear interpolation alone is insufficient for minification. [Advanced High-Quality Filtering](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iii-high-quality-rendering/chapter-27-advanced-high-quality-filtering)

Projector defocus can vary across scene depth. Zhang and Nayar model this as a spatially varying blur and propose measured compensation; that does not establish such calibration in this app. Our implication is to judge both the most compressed mesh region and physical focus across the chosen wall, rather than invent one universal minimum feature width. [Projection Defocus Analysis for Scene Capture and Image Display](https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/Statics/publications/pdfs/Zhang_TOG06.pdf)

Before selecting production: inspect the complete moving loop at target output and quarter size, extreme aspect/warped mesh, lifted blacks and overlap modes; freeze representative silhouette/reveal/closure frames. Then measure actual frame timing with visible output plus preview, one and four overlapping surfaces, on this Radeon 840M. Existing timer-query plumbing (`src/canvas.cpp:137-143,233`) can inform the later measurement, but historical numbers in DEVELOPMENT are not evidence for these proposals. A physical projector test remains necessary for contrast, focus and scene readability. No native-versus-video performance winner, polished-quality score or projector acceptance is claimed here.
