# 13 — Transfiguration: observed material change and the Crown transfer

15 October 2026. Specialist 13. Bounded follow-up to the specific motion-evidence gap in `11-source-audit.md`; read `03-motion.md` and that audit. Original artist film inspected through the live browser player. No product code, generated animation, artwork download, redistribution, benchmark, viewer experiment or physical projection.

## Finding

**The sampled sequence supports coarse body organization plus changing walk poses, while contradicting a literal invariant outer silhouette. It does not establish which cue causes identity perception most strongly.** Head, shoulder/torso and lower-limb regions remain roughly ordered, but their visible boundaries, gaps and material radically change. A water sheet stretches behind the body; later flames conceal precise contours. Consequently, “the same silhouette landmarks remain visible” is too strong as a description of this reference. Persistent branch/antler landmarks in Crown remain our authored hypothesis.

The largely stationary tree-to-stag proposal removes the artist's explicitly retained action: walking throughout the transformation. This changes the transfer materially. Keep the source as evidence for **an ongoing action carried through material upheaval**, then separately prove whether Crown's proposed anatomical correspondence is sufficient without that action.

## Answer to the audit question

1. **Stable geometry landmarks? At a coarse level, yes; at a precise silhouette level, no.** A head above a torso above leg/contact regions remains available. Surface landmarks, clear limb boundaries and the outer contour do not remain consistently exposed. Neither a fixed face nor an antler-like branching landmark carries the sequence.
2. **Gait despite silhouette disruption? Supported, with limits.** The artist explicitly identifies steady walking as the retained action. The live samples show different limb/contact arrangements before, during and after the change, including the flame state. This is consistent with that account and is stronger evidence than a project paragraph alone. Discrete sampling cannot establish an uninterrupted cadence or exclude a brief unseen hesitation; no frame-by-frame gait analysis was done.
3. **Which is primary? Not isolated by this observation.** Both coarse spatial organization and ongoing action are available together. A single artwork without controlled variants cannot rank their causal contributions. The strongest defensible reading is **coarse anatomical organization plus action continuity, tolerating large surface/silhouette disruption**, rather than “identity survives because the same silhouette landmarks stay visible.”

## Required change to the Crown transfer

Keep the reference, but change the claim attached to it. Suggested synthesis wording:

> Transfiguration carries a walking action through drastic material and contour changes. Our stationary tree-to-stag scene does not retain that continuous gait, so its branch-to-antler and trunk-to-torso correspondences are a separate design hypothesis to test.

For a later authorized low-detail correspondence animatic, make the uncertain mechanism reviewable: show one large branch fork becoming the crown/antlers, the trunk becoming the torso, and one ground anchor becoming a hoof without an exposed double-image dissolve. Compare that stationary version against one with a continuous, restrained action through the change, such as a whole-body weight shift or head/torso arc. The comparison should ask whether the viewer tracks **one entity**, before adding texture, fog, glow or fear claims. These are proposed test variants, not findings or a request to start production.

Do not claim that this film proves Crown's recognition, dread, seamless 24-second reset, laptop performance or wall readability. This pass improves the material/occlusion evidence and corrects the transfer; continuous visual motion analysis remains a narrower outstanding limitation.
