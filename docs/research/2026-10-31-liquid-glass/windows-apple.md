# Windows and Apple material research for Windows Liquid Glass 1

Research checked **October 31, 2026**. This document studies Windows 11/Fluent 2 and the design introduced at WWDC25 for macOS Tahoe 26. It proposes a Qt interpretation; it does not establish implementation, performance, or visual acceptance. The existing layout, tools, projector rendering, Classic, and Material remain requirements supplied by the project owner.

## What the primary guidance actually asks for

Microsoft defines material by role: solid surfaces carry most interface content, acrylic provides frosted translucency for temporary surfaces, mica provides an opaque personalized foundation, and smoke establishes modality. This is a hierarchy system, rather than a prescription to blur the whole window. The current [Fluent 2 material guidance](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/material) has no displayed publication date.

[Mica guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/mica), whose metadata reports July 22, 2026, recommends a long-lived window foundation with a separate content layer above it. Mica samples wallpaper once; it is not a continuously transparent view of windows behind the app. Its neutral inactive appearance communicates focus. Microsoft supplies solid fallbacks for high contrast, disabled transparency, battery saving, unsupported systems, and constrained hardware. These are useful behavior models for a projector editor sharing graphics resources with its output.

[Acrylic guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/acrylic), updated October 2, 2026, describes background, blur, exclusion blending, tint, and noise working together. It warns against adjacent acrylic panes, stacked acrylic, and large desktop-acrylic backgrounds. Persistent vertical panes should generally be opaque; overlapping navigation and transient menus are stronger candidates. Custom tint or opacity changes require renewed contrast checks, and accent-colored small text over acrylic is discouraged. This argues for one coherent material per surface rather than independently frosting every field and group.

Windows uses a base/content relationship, contours, and purposeful shadows to communicate hierarchy. The [elevation guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/layering), updated February 19, 2026, warns that excessive shadows create noise. A small control should not cast the same shadow as a dialog. [Fluent motion guidance](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/motion) calls for quick, natural, functional transitions constrained to the element in focus, plus a no-motion setting. Animated decoration elsewhere competes with editing.

Apple's [June 9, 2025 design announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/) identifies real-time reflection, refraction, and specular highlights as defining properties. The [macOS Tahoe announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/macos-tahoe-26-makes-the-mac-more-capable-productive-and-intelligent-than-ever/), also June 9, locates the effect in toolbars, sidebars, navigation, and the Dock while emphasizing familiar content workflows. Its Mac examples are more relevant here than phone lock-screen typography.

The actual transcript of [WWDC25: Meet Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/) explains coordinated optics: edge lensing communicates form; geometry-aware highlights define silhouettes; shadows strengthen over text; tint and dynamic range adapt to the backdrop. Larger surfaces become perceptually thicker, with stronger separation. Small elements can change light/dark appearance; large menus and sidebars avoid that distracting flip. Motion connects a popup to its originating control, and interaction briefly energizes the material. The session explicitly discourages glass-on-glass. It also says Regular and Clear should not be mixed. These are design-system behaviors; a static gradient with a bright border reproduces only a small part of them.

[Apple HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), updated September 9, 2025 after its June 9 introduction, distinguishes the functional glass layer from the content layer and asks designers to use custom glass sparingly. Regular adjusts background blur and luminosity for readable labels and substantial text. Clear prioritizes media visibility. For bright content, HIG suggests considering a dark layer at 35% opacity; already-dark content or AVKit controls providing their own dimming can need none. The launch talk's stronger dimming requirement should therefore be read alongside this later, conditional guidance. That percentage is contextual advice, not a universal Qt recipe.

[WWDC25: Get to know the new design system](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/) supplies an important desktop distinction: compact Mac controls retain rounded rectangles, including dense inspectors; large and extra-large controls adopt capsules for emphasis. Nested shapes use concentric geometry: inner radii and margins relate to their parent. Floating controls should have clear functional groups, and menus should remain spatially connected to their source. Copying phone-sized capsules into every desktop row would discard this density guidance.

## Recommended interpretation for Potato Mapper

The target is a **visibly glossy, optical, Regular-inspired whole-app system**. Glass should be recognizable across the persistent shell, not confined to menus. Stable dark beds protect text inside the glass containers. The following is a proposed translation, not a claim about the current code:

| Surface | Material and interaction direction |
|---|---|
| Main window, title/command region | Cool charcoal/icy glass with a continuous spatial reflection field, clearly curved specular highlights, and a visible optical rim. Distinguish active/inactive windows without continual brightness changes. |
| Persistent media/surface lists and inspector | Visibly thick glass dock shells with luminous edges and shared reflections. Give dense text and numeric fields stable dark beds within them. Retain compact rounded controls, existing row density, and explicit focus/selection. |
| Toolbar groups and important actions | A shared glass plane per functional group. Use visible edge thickness, controlled refractive detail, a soft underside shadow, and localized interactive highlights. Reserve stronger tint for a primary action or selected state. |
| Menus, combo popups, tooltips, dialogs | Consistent raised surfaces with readable contrast and appropriate elevation. Keep inner rows as simple fills on the parent material. Anchor transient interactions to their trigger. |
| Canvas and mapping controls | Preserve exact artwork, handles, and coordinate readability. Avoid dimming or refracting the working image merely to make chrome look glassy. Keep the projector output independent of editor styling. |
| Optional media transport overlay | Explore Clear only as a separate, tightly bounded media context with bold foreground controls and validated dimming. Begin the editor with one Regular-inspired family. |

The intended character combines obvious gloss and depth with clean geometry. Reflections should connect the app spatially; an endless animated sheen would compete with mapping. A backdrop should visibly influence any effect advertised as live refraction. Exact colors, radii, highlight width, blur strength, and durations remain prototype decisions to judge against the real app.

Proposed architecture: define one theme-wide optical coordinate space and material recipe, then paint a shared reflection field through the title area, toolbar, dock headers, and dock shells. Compose each shell from a tinted body, bounded backdrop blur, edge lensing, curved highlights, and one elevation shadow. Paint fields and list rows as stable fills inside that parent, avoiding another transparent glass pass. Cache the environment/backdrop and invalidate it on relevant content, geometry, or interaction changes; avoid perpetual decoration timers. Keep sampling away from the independent projector output and prevent recursive self-capture. This is an implementation hypothesis requiring a working Qt proof.

## Platform limits and acceptance evidence

[Apple's custom-view API guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views) exposes SwiftUI `glassEffect`, interactive response, effect containers, and coordinated morphing. It cautions that too many independent effects degrade performance. Those Apple framework facilities do not become available to Qt Widgets on Windows through a stylesheet. Similarly, Microsoft's native backdrop resources do not automatically style every Qt child control. A Qt implementation must own its painting, sampling boundaries, event behavior, and fallback policy. Painted rims and gradients are plausible inexpensive approximations; genuine backdrop blur/refraction requires a separate rendering investigation and measured proof.

[Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) asks for contrast measurement, information beyond color alone, and reduced motion. For this theme, test normal text against a 4.5:1 target over actual composited backgrounds; provide explicit focus/selection outlines, opaque reduced-transparency/high-contrast appearances, and a motion-free path. Verify black, white, saturated, checkerboard, and moving media. Compare enabled effects against the baseline while editing and playing projector output. Neither attractive stills nor framework documentation establishes frame-time cost, keyboard usability, or physical-projector correctness.
